Screening aid only. No label here certifies that water may be drunk. "Within limits" covers arsenic, manganese and sodium only; bacteria, nitrate and everything else are not in the data. The survey is from 1998-99.
"Not ruled out" is weak: in held-out testing about 59% of wells labelled this way were not within limits. Treat it as a reason to send a sample to a laboratory, nothing more. Most answers are "unknown" or "no window", and that is the honest result.
Each square is a 0.05-degree cell (about 5 km), computed at its centre. Straight north-south or east-west edges come from the model's splits on latitude and longitude, not from mapped geology. Outlines are approximate.

P(within) is the model's probability that all three parameters are within the chosen limits. The label comes from conformal thresholds, not from P alone being high or low. "Limiting" is the parameter with the lowest single-parameter probability (As arsenic, Mn manganese, Na high sodium, possible salinity concern); it is descriptive, not a conformal label. Depths below 150 m are always "unknown" in this tool: the deep calibration wells were too few to exclude either outcome, which says nothing about the water there. For an exact point use step6_profile.py.

Probably not within limits

The model excludes "within limits" at this depth. In testing it caught about 97% of wells that were truly over a limit with this label or "unknown".

Not ruled out

The model cannot exclude "within limits". Rare (about 1% of cells at 10-20 m) and weak evidence. A lead for a laboratory test only.

Unknown

The model cannot decide. This is the most common label. Nothing is filled in between depths.

Window

A run of consecutive "not ruled out" depths on the grid 10, 20, 30, 50, 75, 100, 150 m. "No window" is the usual answer. Window edges are uncertain by one grid step.

Below 150 m (not judged by the model)

This note comes from the survey summary and published studies, not from the model. High arsenic is rare below 150 m: in this survey 0.9% of the 326 deep wells were above 50 µg/L. But 49% of them had sodium above 200 mg/L, mostly in the coastal south, and heavy deep pumping can pull arsenic down over years. A deep well still needs a laboratory test and regular re-testing.
Data: BGS and DPHE. 2001. Arsenic contamination of groundwater in Bangladesh. Kinniburgh, D G, and Smedley, P L (editors). British Geological Survey Technical Report WC/00/19. (Nottingham, UK: British Geological Survey.) Model: Step 5 saved tool, variant strat_u01 (depth-stratified conformal thresholds, unsafe class at 99% target coverage). Strict = arsenic 10 µg/L, manganese 0.08 mg/L, sodium 200 mg/L; Local = arsenic 50 µg/L, manganese 0.1 mg/L, sodium 200 mg/L. Sodium results are "high sodium (possible salinity concern)". Sodium above 200 mg/L can come from seawater-related salinity or from fresh sodium-bicarbonate water; the survey has no chloride, so this tool cannot tell which.
